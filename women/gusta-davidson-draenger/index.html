Resister · 1917-1943

Gusta Davidson Draenger

In a death cell she wrote her story on scraps of paper and made sure it left the prison before she did.

From Akiva to the underground

Gusta Davidson Draenger was born in Kraków in 1917 into a Gur Hasidic family. She joined the youth movement Akiva, served on its central committee, edited its paper Zeirim and kept its archive. She was arrested in September 1939 with Shimshon Draenger and released by bribe. They married in early 1940.

He-Halutz ha-Lohem

In August 1942 the Kraków movements merged into He-Halutz ha-Lohem, under Dolek Liebeskind and Shimshon Draenger. On 22 December 1942 the group attacked the Cyganeria café as part of a day of coordinated actions.

The death cell

She was arrested on 18 January 1943. Held in the death cell from February to April 1943, she wrote Pamiętnik Justyny (Justyna's Narrative) on scraps of paper, including toilet paper, and had it copied out in four sets.

Escape and capture

On 29 April 1943 she escaped from a transport toward Płaszów. The remnant of the group printed its paper from Bochnia and Nowy Wiśnicz. She was recaptured on 8 November 1943 near the Hungarian border. She and Shimshon did not survive. She was 26.

Her words survive

Her narrative was published in Polish in 1946 and in Hebrew in 1953 by the Ghetto Fighters' House. The English edition, Justyna's Narrative, edited by Eli Pfefferkorn and David H. Hirsch, appeared in 1996 from the University of Massachusetts Press.
