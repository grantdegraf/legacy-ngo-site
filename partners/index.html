The case for support

A completed body of verified history, ready to reach classrooms

Women Without Fear gathers the researched biographies of thirty-six Jewish women who resisted the Nazi regime between 1933 and 1945 — fighters, rescuers, forgers and witnesses. Every subject is drawn from the verified historical record: archival evidence, survivor testimony and published scholarship. No composites, no invention. The manuscript is complete and preparing for publication; a documentary series is in development.

What the project needs now is the means to finish it properly and put it into the hands of the people who will teach from it. That is what a partnership funds.

Antisemitism is rising globally — and institutions need historically reliable material to answer it.

The last direct witnesses are passing, taking living memory with them.

Jewish women's resistance remains underrepresented in mainstream historical accounts and in classroom material.

Story and film reach young audiences where a textbook does not — and the work is built for both.

What a partnership can fund

Support is directed to defined outcomes, agreed in advance

Manuscript completion and publication

Final editorial work, permissions and rights clearance, and the costs of bringing Women Without Fear to publication.

Educational resource development

Teaching guides, discussion material and classroom-ready adaptations built from the verified source record.

Books for students, classrooms and museums

Funded copies placed with schools, educators and cultural institutions rather than sold into them.

Geographic distribution programmes

Concentrated distribution into a defined region, school district, country or institutional network.

Translation and curriculum development

Translation into a target language, and adaptation of the material to a national curriculum framework.

Lectures, presentations and events

Author presentations, screenings and educational events hosted with schools, museums and community organisations.

These are the categories a partnership can be built around. Any figures — numbers of books, schools, territories or participants — are set as goals in the written partnership agreement and are described as goals, not as results already achieved. The Legacy Project does not publish counts of students reached, books distributed, participating museums or confirmed partners that it cannot evidence.

Indicative levels

Partnership levels are starting points for a conversation

The levels below indicate the scale at which each kind of programme becomes possible. They are not products, and there is no checkout. Scope, timing, outcomes and recognition are settled in discussion and recorded in a written agreement.

Founding Partnerfrom $25,000

Supports manuscript completion and publication, or the development of a defined educational resource.

Education Partnerfrom $50,000

Funds an educational programme: classroom materials together with the placement of books in schools and museums.

National Distribution Partnerfrom $100,000

Underwrites distribution into a defined country, region or institutional network, with an agreed delivery plan.

Lead Legacy Partner$150,000 and above

Supports the project as a whole across publication, education and distribution, over a multi-year horizon.

Bespoke Partnershipdeveloped around agreed outcomes

Built from the outcomes an organisation wants to achieve — a language, a territory, a curriculum, an institution — rather than from a figure.

Foundations and institutions working to their own grant cycles, application formats or reporting requirements are welcome to say so at the outset; the project works to the funder's process.

Leadership gifts from individuals and families

A leadership gift of $10,000 or more can support a named educational or distribution initiative — a translation, a school programme, a region. Leadership gifts are discussed in the same way as institutional partnerships, and the same governance terms apply.

Recognition and governance

What recognition can look like, and what it never affects

Recognition developed by agreement

Depending on the partnership, recognition may be discussed in forms such as:

  • Acknowledgement on legacy.ngo
  • Recognition in materials produced by The Legacy Project
  • Naming of a defined initiative, such as a distribution programme, a translation or an educational resource
  • Invitations to lectures, screenings and educational events
  • Written reporting on the outcomes the partnership funded

What is not offered in advance

So that nothing is promised that cannot be honoured:

  • Recognition inside a traditionally published edition of the book cannot be committed in advance, because it may require the publisher's agreement
  • No partnership carries editorial control, approval rights over historical content, or the right to review the text before publication
  • Exclusivity, category exclusivity and territorial rights are not offered as standard and exist only where separately agreed in writing
  • No partner name or logo is displayed by The Legacy Project without documented written permission
  • Tax treatment is a matter for the fiscal sponsor and your own advisers, not for this page

Partnership recognition is developed by agreement. Historical integrity and editorial independence remain with The Legacy Project.
