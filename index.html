What Women Without Fear is

Read Róża Robota’s chapter

A completed collection of 36 biographical narratives of resistance, rescue and remembrance, drawing on archival evidence, survivor testimony and published scholarship. The subjects include Jewish women and non-Jewish rescuers. A documentary series based on their lives is in development; educational adaptations are being developed for schools, museums and community groups.

Meet the women

Four lives. Different forms of resistance.

  • Auschwitz-Birkenau

    Róża Robota

    1921–1945

    At Auschwitz-Birkenau, Róża Robota helped organise the clandestine transfer of gunpowder from women working in the munitions factory to prisoners preparing resistance. The explosives supported the Sonderkommando revolt of 7 October 1944. Her contribution shows how cooperation and resolve could sustain resistance even within a killing centre.

    Read her chapter Historical reference: US Holocaust Memorial Museum

  • Bratislava

    Gisi Fleischmann

    1892–1944

    In Bratislava, Gisi Fleischmann helped lead the Working Group, an underground network that sought to protect Jews facing deportation. She helped raise funds, organise rescue efforts and negotiate with officials in an attempt to save lives. Her story reveals resistance through leadership, persistence and the difficult work of rescue.

    Read the historical background at Yad Vashem

  • Warsaw Ghetto

    Rachel Auerbach

    1903–1976

    In the Warsaw ghetto, writer Rachel Auerbach contributed to the clandestine Oneg Shabbat archive, documenting Jewish life under Nazi persecution. By recording what she witnessed, she helped preserve a historical record the occupiers sought to erase. Her contribution demonstrates that testimony and the defence of memory were also forms of resistance.

    Read the historical background at the US Holocaust Memorial Museum

  • Partisan resistance

    Faye Schulman

    1919–2021

    After surviving the destruction of her community, photographer Faye Schulman joined Soviet partisans. She served as a nurse and photographed life and resistance in the forests. Her images preserve evidence of Jewish participation in the partisan struggle, bringing together the work of survival, care and historical witness.

    Read the historical background at the US Holocaust Memorial Museum

Why now

The window to preserve these stories is closing

Antisemitism is rising globally — and communities need historically reliable material to answer it.

The last direct witnesses are passing, taking living memory with them.

Jewish women's resistance remains underrepresented in mainstream historical accounts.

Young audiences respond to story and film — among the strongest tools for memory transmission.

The founder

Grant de Graf

Writer and filmmaker, and the author of Women Without Fear. He brings a filmmaker's discipline to the work — rigorous sourcing, cinematic craft, and the conviction that honoring courage means telling these stories with the same precision the people in them brought to their own lives.

Thirty-six stories filled a book; thousands more remain documented and largely untold. The Legacy Project grew out of that work.
